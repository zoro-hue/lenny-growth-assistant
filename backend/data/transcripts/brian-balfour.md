# Brian Balfour on the Four Fits Framework and Why Growth Is a System | Episode 112

## Transcript

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:00:10):
Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Brian Balfour, founder and CEO of Reforge and former VP of Growth at HubSpot. Brian is the architect behind many of the core growth frameworks used by modern technology operators. Brian, it's great to have you.

**Brian Balfour** (00:00:28):
Great to be here, Lenny!

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:00:32):
Brian, your most influential essay series is the Four Fits Framework. Most founders only obsess over Product-Market Fit. Why is Product-Market Fit by itself insufficient to build a venture-scale company?

**Brian Balfour** (00:00:48):
Product-Market Fit is only 25% of the equation. In order to build a $100 million+ ARR company, you need Four Fits that all reinforce each other:
1. Market-Product Fit
2. Product-Channel Fit
3. Channel-Model Fit
4. Model-Market Fit
You cannot think of these in isolation. They are constantly shifting gears in an engine.

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:01:18):
Can you dive into Product-Channel Fit? You have a famous quote that products are built to fit channels, not the other way around. What does that mean?

**Brian Balfour** (00:01:28):
Distribution channels—whether it is Google SEO, Facebook ads, Apple App Store, or Virality—do not care about your product. You do not control the rules of Google or Meta; they control their algorithms. Therefore, you cannot build a product and then just 'find a channel.' The characteristics of your product must be designed specifically to exploit the natural dynamics of the channel. For example, if you want SEO to be your primary channel, your product must naturally generate millions of unique, high-intent indexable pages as users interact with it.

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:02:12):
What about Channel-Model Fit? How does your pricing model dictate your acquisition channels?

**Brian Balfour** (00:02:20):
Your pricing model determines what CAC you can afford. If your Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) is $10 a month, you cannot use an outbound sales team or expensive enterprise marketing—the math simply will not work. Low ARPU products require low CAC distribution channels like virality, user-generated content, or organic search. Conversely, high ARPU products ($100k+ annual contracts) can afford direct sales, high-touch onboarding, and multi-month sales cycles. Companies get trapped in the 'growth danger zone' when their ARPU is too high for self-serve but too low to support an enterprise sales team.
