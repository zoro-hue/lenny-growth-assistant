# Rahul Vohra on The Product-Market Fit Engine and Building Superhuman | Episode 15

## Transcript

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:00:10):
Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Rahul Vohra, founder and CEO of Superhuman. Rahul created what is widely considered the gold standard methodology for measuring and driving product-market fit. Rahul, welcome to the show.

**Rahul Vohra** (00:00:28):
Thank you Lenny, fantastic to be here.

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:00:32):
Rahul, in 2017 you had been building Superhuman for two years, and you felt the existential terror that every founder feels: how do we actually know if we have product-market fit? Sean Ellis had proposed the 40% rule, but how did you turn that into an operational engine?

**Rahul Vohra** (00:00:52):
We surveyed our users and only 22% said they would be very disappointed if Superhuman disappeared. Instead of despairing, we created a systematic method to drive that number up to 58%. The key is:
1. Segment respondents: Ask 'How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?' (Very disappointed, Somewhat disappointed, Not disappointed).
2. Double down on your advocates: Analyze the 'Very disappointed' cohort to identify your high-expectation customer (HXC) archetype.
3. Filter the middle group: Look at 'Somewhat disappointed' users, but ONLY those who share the same primary benefit as your 'Very disappointed' group. Ignore the rest.
4. Allocate roadmap 50/50: Dedicate half of your engineering bandwidth to doubling down on what your fans love, and the other half to addressing the specific objections of that target middle group.

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:02:15):
Why is it so critical to ignore the 'Somewhat disappointed' users whose primary benefit differed from the lovers?

**Rahul Vohra** (00:02:24):
Because if you listen to everyone, you will build a Frankenstein product. Users who want completely different value propositions will pull your product in directions that dilute what makes it magical for your core archetype. You must politely ignore feedback from users who do not fit your core persona.
