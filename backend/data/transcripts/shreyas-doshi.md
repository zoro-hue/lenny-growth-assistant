**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:00:34):
Shreyas, let's start with your famous LNO framework. Product managers are notorious for feeling perpetually overwhelmed by their inboxes and backlogs. How does the LNO framework solve this?

**Shreyas Doshi** (00:00:48):
The LNO framework classifies every task on your plate into three distinct buckets:
1. **L: Leverage tasks:** High-impact tasks where doing a 10x job creates a 10x difference in company trajectory. Examples include product strategy, core roadmap decisions, hiring key leaders, or running customer problem discovery.
2. **N: Neutral tasks:** Essential operational tasks where doing a good job (1x) is required, but doing a 10x job yields zero additional value. Examples include weekly project status updates or sprint grooming.
3. **O: Overhead tasks:** Necessary chores where you should deliberately aim for a 'B-' or 'C+' level of effort just to get them done quickly. Examples include expense reports, routine administrative forms, or scheduling.
The fatal error most product managers make is applying 10x perfectionism to Neutral and Overhead tasks, which leaves them too exhausted to do deep work on Leverage tasks.

**Lenny Rachitsky** (00:02:05):
You also talk extensively about 'High Agency' in product operators. What defines a high-agency product manager?

**Shreyas Doshi** (00:02:14):
High agency is the ability to bend reality to achieve a desirable outcome despite immense constraints, lack of authority, or institutional inertia. A low-agency operator complains about lack of executive alignment, missing engineering resources, or bureaucratic hurdles. A high-agency operator asks: 'Given these exact constraints, what creative maneuver or prototype can I build today that proves value and moves the needle?' High agency is the single highest predictor of long-term product impact.
